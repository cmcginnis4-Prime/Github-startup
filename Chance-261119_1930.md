- **Goal:** Find out how far text prompts alone keep a character on-model.

- **Tools:** Nano Banana, ChatGPT, Milanote

- **Output:** A completed Drift Test entry in the Prompt Log and Experiment Log, with screenshots.

- **Success indicator:** All 4 panels are scored on all 3 criteria, and you can name which criterion drifted first.

**Week 6: Reference images rung · Dates TBC**

- **Goal:** Test whether reference images fix what prompts alone couldn't.

- **Activities:** Build a simple character reference sheet (front, side, key expressions). Rerun the same 4 panels with the sheet attached. Respect each tool's reference limit (see Source 2). Score with the same rubric and compare against Week 5.

- **Tools:** Nano Banana, ChatGPT, Milanote

- **Output:** A reference sheet, plus a side-by-side Week 5 vs. Week 6 comparison card.

- **Success indicator:** Scores improve on at least one criterion, _or_ you can explain why they didn't. A failed rung is still data.

## Project Identity Card

**Section 1: Project Identity Card**

**Title**\
Keeping AI On-Model: Directing a Comic

**Curiosity statement**\
How far can prompts alone keep an AI-illustrated comic on-model, and when do I need references or templates to stay in control?

**Short Summary**

AI image tools let me make a comic without hand-drawing every panel, but they tend to quietly redesign characters from one image to the next. I'm testing a ladder of control methods (text prompts → reference images → panel templates → AI-friendly character designs) in Nano Banana and ChatGPT to find how much direction it takes to keep characters consistent, especially in mostly silent panels where an expression carries the story.

**Audience / context**\
Creators who can design a character but can't hand-illustrate every panel, like indie comic makers and design students, who want to know when prompts stop being enough. 

**Possible final directions**

1. **Lead: Consistency playbook.** The control ladder, prompt templates, and reference sheets, with notes on when each is needed.

3. **Supporting: Tool showdown.** The same character and panels in both tools, scored on the Drift Test rubric. Off-model failures double as a blooper reel. _(Pending instructor feedback on one tool vs. two.)_

## Inquiry Brief

**Research question**\
How much direction does it take to keep an AI-illustrated comic character on-model across panels, and at what point does each control method (text prompts, reference images, panel templates, AI-friendly character design) become necessary?

**Why it matters**\
For creators short on time or budget, AI can stand in for hand-illustration. The catch is drift: a character who changes faces between panels breaks the comic, and in silent sight gags the expression _is_ the joke. Knowing which control step a scene actually needs saves wasted generations and keeps the creator in charge of the vision.

**What I need to learn**

- Where each tool drifts first: art style, face and expressions, or props and outfit

- How far text prompts alone get before drift becomes unacceptable

- What reference images and panel templates fix, and what they don't

- Which character design traits AI keeps reliably and which it loses

- What each control step costs in time and effort, since that's the audience's real budget

- How much drift is acceptable _(pending instructor feedback)_

**Possible final outcomes**

1. **Consistency playbook:** the control ladder, prompt templates, and reference sheets, with notes on when each one is needed.

2. **Short web comic:** a mostly silent sci-fi comedy made with the workflow, as proof that it works.

3. **Tool showdown:** the same character and panels in Nano Banana and ChatGPT, scored on the Drift Test rubric, with a blooper reel of off-model failures.
