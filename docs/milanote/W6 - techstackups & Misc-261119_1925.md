On top of the information I discovered, NotebookLM also likes to try its hand at recreating images found in articles. It does an okay job of capturing the main ideas, but when it comes to replicating specific details, it’s willing to fill in the gaps with its own judgment. For example, in the article, the first test involved a man with tattoos. In NotebookLM’s recreation, it was able to decently replicate what was described in the article. However, because the article never explicitly said something along the lines of “the guy” or provided any context to establish the subject’s gender, it made its own guess and turned the model into a woman. This isn’t really relevant to the actual content of the article, but it’s just something interesting I noticed.

Actually getting into the tests, I discovered that they were mostly standard evaluations measuring how quickly the different models could respond to prompts and how accurately they could follow them. Overall, Runway seemed to be the worst-performing model, failing all of the tests. ChatGPT did an acceptable job, while Gemini performed well for a budget model. The only one that really stood out was Flux 2. It was able to accurately add clothing to a model and place that model into a scene more accurately than the others.
